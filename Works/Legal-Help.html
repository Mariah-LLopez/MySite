Overview

Helping first-time legal help seekers feel prepared before a consultation

This learning module is designed for individuals seeking legal advice for the first time who are unsure how to prepare.

The module walks learners through practical preparation steps, including what documents to gather, how to organize a timeline, and how to identify useful questions before meeting with a lawyer.

Problem & Goals

Making the first legal consultation less confusing and more productive

People navigating legal situations for the first time often do not know what to bring, what to say, or what questions to ask.

This lack of preparation can lead to incomplete consultations, higher costs, and increased anxiety. The goal was to demystify the preparation process and help learners organize information before meeting with an attorney.

Document Readiness

Help learners identify documents, correspondence, identification, and records that may be relevant to their situation.

Timeline Clarity

Guide learners through creating a simple written sequence of events before their appointment.

Better Questions

Support learners in identifying what they need to ask the attorney and what information the attorney may need from them.

User Scenario

Need Legal Help

A learner is preparing to speak with an attorney for the first time and feels unsure about what to expect.

Gather Documents

The learner identifies documents, messages, notices, records, or identification that may help explain the situation.

Build a Timeline

The learner organizes key events into a simple sequence so the attorney can understand what happened.

Prepare Questions

The learner writes down questions about next steps, costs, deadlines, risks, and what information the attorney needs.

Attend Consultation

The learner arrives more organized, communicates more clearly, and uses consultation time more effectively.

My Role

Designing a plain-language learning sequence for a broad public audience

As the instructional designer, I identified the key informational gaps that first-time legal consultation seekers face and structured those needs into a brief, plain-language module.

I wrote all module content, designed the interactive checklist activities, and developed the full module in Articulate 360 with accessibility and plain language as guiding principles throughout.

Discovery & Constraints

Creating a helpful module that works across different legal situations

Content development drew on commonly available public legal resources, self-help legal guides, and general patterns of what attorneys often request from new clients.

The primary constraint was audience breadth. The module needed to be relevant across different types of legal situations, including family law, landlord-tenant, employment, and estate matters, without becoming too general to be useful.

Broad Audience

The module needed to support many legal situations without giving situation-specific legal advice.

Plain Language

Content needed to remain accessible for learners with different reading levels and comfort with legal terms.

Practical Focus

The module needed to emphasize preparation steps learners could complete before a consultation.

Key Features

Interactive Document Checklist

The checklist activity allows learners to mark off document types that may be relevant to their legal situation before meeting with an attorney.

Timeline Reflection Prompts

Reflection prompts guide learners in organizing key dates, events, notices, conversations, and decisions into a simple written timeline.

Printable Preparation Checklist

The module closes with a printable checklist learners can bring to their consultation or use as a final preparation guide.

Information Architecture & User Flows

Structuring the module around the first consultation preparation process

The module is organized into three short sections: what to bring, building your timeline, and questions to ask.

The learner moves through the sections in sequence, completing a brief reflection prompt at the end of each section before reaching the printable preparation checklist.

Primary Learner Flow

What to Bring → Interactive Document Checklist → Building Your Timeline → Questions to Ask → Printable Preparation Checklist → Completion

Wireframes & Prototypes

Storyboarding each screen for readability and accessibility

A storyboard outlined each screen’s content, interaction type, and plain language review notes before development in Articulate 360.

The storyboard was reviewed for readability and accessibility, with a Flesch-Kincaid target of Grade 6–8 applied to all instructional copy.

Requirements & Acceptance Criteria

Making legal preparation accessible to non-specialist learners

The module was designed to be completable in under 15 minutes with no prior legal knowledge assumed.

Accessibility requirements included keyboard-navigable interactions, alt text on all visual elements, sufficient color contrast, and screen reader compatibility. Plain language criteria were applied to every content screen.

Analytics, Iteration, Outcomes & Next Steps

Improving clarity through plain-language review

The module was reviewed for plain language compliance and clarity by a peer with no legal background.

Feedback produced one revision cycle that simplified the document-gathering section and added brief explanatory notes for legal terms that could not be entirely avoided.
